# Code of conduct

## Our pledge

We want Apsis to be a place where anyone can learn about IAM escalation
analysis, ask a basic question, or disagree about a design decision without
being made to feel unwelcome. Participation is open regardless of experience,
identity, background, or employer.

## Expected behaviour

- Be direct about the work and generous with the person. Critique the claim,
  the code, or the evidence, not the contributor.
- Assume a question is honest. If the docs made something unclear, the docs are
  part of the bug.
- Respect scope. A security tool deals in real accounts; never ask someone to
  share credentials, account exports, or customer data to make a point. Use the
  synthetic fixtures.
- Give credit. A route, a fixture, or a fix that came from someone else keeps
  their name on it.
- Accept a maintained "no". A declined pull request is a decision about the
  project's shape, not a judgement of the person who sent it.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption in issues, pull requests,
  reviews, or any project space.
- Discriminatory language or imagery, including sexualised attention.
- Publishing someone's private information, credentials, or account details.
- Presenting another person's work as your own, or stripping attribution from a
  fixture or design.
- Using the project to plan or carry out unauthorised access to a system.

## Scope

This code applies in the repository, its issues and pull requests, and any space
where someone is representing the project.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report a concern privately to the maintainer, **@Meruyert5**, through GitHub. Do
not open a public issue for a conduct report. Reports are read in good faith and
kept confidential.

The maintainer may edit or remove a comment, close a thread, reject a
contribution, or temporarily or permanently restrict participation. Enforcement
is proportional to the behaviour and its impact. A person asked to stop is
expected to stop.
