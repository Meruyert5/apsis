The scene is the instrument.

Every deck opens on a real WebGL scene. The geometry is shared; the material language is not. This is the 3D contract that keeps it a tool.

Camera

38° perspective, 12.6 units, look-at primary. Drag with damping; scroll 0.7–1.7×; double-click resets.

Surface

Matte bone and gold, thin lines, no bloom. The star is the only glow; the route is the only saturated line.

Labels

Perimeter rail, four labels max. A leader tick, no floating text. Hide before the labels stack.

Fallback

2D SVG on the same plate. Pause offscreen, respect reduced motion, keep the final frame.

22s orbit / 1.4s hold

The comet is the only ambient loop. The star breathes at 5.6s. The light curve and the waveform are the only things that play on demand.

What makes it hold.

The rules are the difference between an authored system and a nice screenshot.

Do

  • One ink per act; gold is the shared apsis.
  • Hairlines and empty space before cards and shadows.
  • Real figure captions under every instrument.
  • Serif for display, sans for reading, mono for data.
  • Let the 3D scene be a plate, not a background.

Don't

  • No gradient panels, glass cards or glowing buttons.
  • No pill controls or 24px radii.
  • No floating labels over the orrery.
  • No paragraph in the display serif.
  • No five-accent rainbow.
