Brief 01 / a flagship, not a template

Five ways to make an account feel like an instrument.

We are not picking a colour. We are picking the register a security leader trusts, the density an operator can read, and the thing a visitor remembers after the demo. Five complete systems follow, each with a real WebGL stage, a 2D fallback and a reduced-motion path.

5 directions
1 choice

Each is a design system plus a working UI kit, built on the same Apsis data: 16 accounts, 194 identities, 144 routes, 37 edges cut.

Open each direction at 1440 and 390.

01 / the room

Four people, five bets, one argument.

The brief crossed the table the way it usually does: the CEO wants a flagship, the CTO wants it to survive production, the engineer wants it to be buildable, and the designer wants it to mean something.

Cast Mara / CEO Ilya / CTO Priya / engineer Noa / designer Round 01 the brief 02 the five 03 the call

“It looks like every other AI security dashboard. We sell trust to people who have seen a hundred of these. The site should feel like a flagship instrument, not a template with our logo on it.”

IlyaCTO

“A flagship has to survive sixteen fixtures, four 3D scenes, reduced motion, a 390px screen and a 14px floor. I do not want a design that only works in the hero and falls apart in the readout.”

“No new runtime dependency, no layout thrash, one focus system. If the type needs a remote font, we self-host it. And I want the tokens to be boring enough that another engineer can extend a panel without asking Noa.”

“Then we need one idea per direction, not five features. I will bring five systems that make different bets: editorial, cyber, Swiss, organic, terminal. Same product, same data, five different arguments about what a security instrument should feel like.”

“Which one says ‘this is the category’?”

N
Noadesigner

“Orbital Ops. It is the most direct heir to the product: four decks, live instruments, glass, signal. It is also the easiest one to overdo. My restraint rule: glow only where a state is live, glass only on the hero and one readout.”

IlyaCTO

“And which one do I trust in a procurement review?”

N
Noadesigner

“Swiss Instrument. Paper, two inks, a visible grid, a real diagram with numbered labels. It reads like an instrument manual, not a sci-fi film. It is the most legible and the least likely to age badly.”

P
Priyaengineer

“The light one is also the cheapest to build: no glass, no blur, no WebGL glow budget. The terminal is the other extreme — fastest to build, most opinionated. It is the engineer-love option, not the buyer option.”

M
MaraCEO

“Good. Build all five. I want to see the product in each, not a moodboard. Then we will pick with our eyes, not with a vote.”

Designer’s note. The five are not skins of one layout. Each has its own grid, type pairing, surface logic, component shape and motion budget. The comparison is only useful if the bones differ.

03 / how to review
  1. Read the coverDoes the thesis sound like the company you want to be? Each direction states its bet on the first screen.
  2. Use the kitDrag the 3D stage, use the 2D button, click the tabs, resize to 390. The kit is the product in miniature, not a poster.
  3. Check the trade-offEvery direction lists what it wins and what it loses. Pick the loss you can live with.

Recommendation from the room. If the launch is category-led and the demo is the product, start with 02 Orbital Ops. If the buyer is a CISO or a procurement committee, start with 03 Swiss Instrument. The designer would keep 01 The Observatory as the brand layer and use 02 for the console. The final call is yours.
